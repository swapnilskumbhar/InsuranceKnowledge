# Study plan — private

**Not published.** This file lives at the repo root deliberately: Astro only serves pages from
`src/content/docs/`, so nothing here appears on the site.

This is the personal learning track extracted from the original single-file guide. It is a
syllabus, with a reader of one. The handbook is a reference work for other engineers — different
audience, different voice — so the two are kept apart on purpose.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Period of study** | 6–9 months, renewable |
| **Territory** | India + global markets |
| **Deductible** | Consistent weekly effort |
| **Exclusions** | Shallow, one-time reading |

## Phase 1 · Weeks 1–4 — Foundations

- Read one fundamentals text end to end: **Vaughan & Vaughan, *Fundamentals of Risk and
  Insurance***, or **Boggs, *Property & Casualty Insurance Concepts Simplified*** (short, practical).
- Or start with **Insurance Institute of India Licentiate** material (IC-01 Principles, IC-02
  Life Practice, IC-11 General Practice) — structured and India-specific.
- Keep a personal glossary; use the IRMI and NAIC glossaries as references.

## Phase 2 · Months 2–6 — Structured certification

- **India:** III's Licentiate → Associateship → Fellowship ladder.
- **Global P&C:** The Institutes' **AINS**, then **CPCU** — even 2–3 papers teach a great deal
  about operations.
- **Life:** **LOMA FLMI** — the global standard for life operations, popular with IT professionals.
- **UK/commercial:** CII's Cert CII → Dip CII.

## Phase 3 · Parallel — Domain in systems

- Vendor training: **Guidewire Education** (free intro courses plus certification tracks),
  Duck Creek University, Salesforce Trailhead insurance modules.
- Read ACORD's public standards overviews; skim ACORD 125/126 commercial application forms to
  see how the industry structures risk data.
- Study the **IRDAI Annual Report** — the best free education on the Indian market's shape.

## Phase 4 · Habit — Live industry awareness

- **News:** Insurance Journal, Reinsurance News, Artemis, Asia Insurance Review, IRDAI circulars.
- **Podcasts:** The Voice of Insurance, Profiles in Risk, the InsTech podcast.
- **YouTube:** The Institutes, LOMA, NPTEL insurance lectures, Guidewire's channel, InsTech event
  recordings, Marsh/Aon/WTW explainers on commercial lines.

## Phase 5 · Synthesis — Architect-level depth

- Pick **one LOB** (motor or health are ideal in India) and map its complete value chain: every
  actor, document, system touchpoint and integration. Repeat later for one commercial line and
  term life. **Three deep maps beat shallow knowledge of twelve LOBs.**
- Practise explaining insurance to non-insurance people — leading BAs means translating in both
  directions.
- Learn to read an insurer's annual report (combined ratio, GWP by LOB, solvency ratio).

---

## How this maps onto writing the handbook

Phase 5 *is* the handbook's Phase 1 build order. Writing a page to the `_TEMPLATE.md` standard —
with a worked quote to the final rupee — is the same work as mapping an LOB properly. The
handbook is the artefact of the study, which is why the two shouldn't be merged into one
document but should stay in step.
