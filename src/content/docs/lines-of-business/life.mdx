Life is the exception to indemnity: these are **benefit contracts**, paying an agreed sum rather
than restoring a loss.

<Accordion title="Term Life" tag="Term">
Pure protection: if the insured dies during the term, beneficiaries receive the sum assured; no
maturity value. The cheapest form of life cover. Underwriting weighs health, lifestyle, and
**financial underwriting** — does the sum assured make sense against income?

<Example>
A 32-year-old buys ₹1 crore of term cover. Death in year 5 → the nominee receives ₹1 crore.
Survive the 30-year term → nothing is paid. That is the deal, and why it is cheap.
</Example>
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Whole Life, Endowment & Money-Back" tag="Savings">
Insurance plus savings. **Whole life** covers the entire lifetime and builds cash value.
**Endowment** pays sum assured plus bonuses at maturity or earlier death; **money-back**
variants pay periodic installments. **Participating** policies share insurer surplus as
bonuses; **non-par** products carry guaranteed benefits.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="ULIPs" tag="ULIP">
Unit-Linked Insurance Plans: premium buys units in customer-chosen funds, with life cover on
top. NAV calculation, fund switches and partial withdrawals make ULIP administration
system-heavy — closer to asset management than to classic insurance.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Annuities & Pension" tag="Pension">
The reverse of life insurance: the customer accumulates a corpus and the insurer pays a
guaranteed income for life. The actuarial challenge is **longevity risk** — people living
longer than priced.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Group Life & Credit Life" tag="Group">
Employer-provided term cover for employees; **credit life** pays off a loan balance if the
borrower dies — a bancassurance staple sold alongside home loans.
</Accordion>

<SystemsNote>
**A life policy outlives multiple system generations.** An endowment sold in 1998 must still pay
its maturity in 2028, with its bonus history intact, after three core-system migrations.

This makes data migration and closed-book administration perennial programmes rather than
one-off projects, and it means the retention requirement on life data is measured in decades,
not years.
</SystemsNote>

<Gaps>
