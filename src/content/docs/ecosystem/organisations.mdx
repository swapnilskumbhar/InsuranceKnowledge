The industry is a chain of distinct organisation types, and software boundaries map closely
onto the boundaries between them. Every one of these is a party in your system — see
[the domain model](/systems/domain-model/) for why that matters.

<Ledger>
  <LedgerRow term="Insurers (carriers)">
    Take on the risk and pay the claims. Everything else in the chain orbits them.
    <Eg>Allianz, AXA, Chubb; in India LIC, ICICI Lombard, HDFC ERGO, New India Assurance, Star Health.</Eg>
  </LedgerRow>
  <LedgerRow term="Reinsurers">
    Insure the insurers, so a catastrophe doesn't sink any one balance sheet.
    <Eg>Munich Re, Swiss Re, Hannover Re, Lloyd's syndicates; in India GIC Re.</Eg>
  </LedgerRow>
  <LedgerRow term="Brokers">
    Represent the **customer**. They shop the market and negotiate terms — dominant in
    commercial insurance.
    <Eg>Marsh McLennan, Aon, WTW, Gallagher.</Eg>
  </LedgerRow>
  <LedgerRow term="Agents">
    Represent the **insurer**. Tied agents sell for one carrier, independent agents for several.
    Dominant in personal lines and Indian life insurance.
  </LedgerRow>
  <LedgerRow term="MGAs / MGUs">
    Intermediaries with **delegated underwriting authority** — they underwrite and issue on a
    carrier's behalf, often in niches like cyber or marine. Fast-growing and integration-hungry.
  </LedgerRow>
  <LedgerRow term="TPAs">
    Third-Party Administrators handle claims or policy admin for the insurer — the backbone of
    Indian health claims.
  </LedgerRow>
  <LedgerRow term="Lloyd's of London">
    Not an insurer but a **marketplace**, where syndicates underwrite specialty and large
    commercial risks via Lloyd's brokers. Central to marine, aviation, energy and cyber.
  </LedgerRow>
  <LedgerRow term="Regulators">
    Approve products, mandate solvency capital, police conduct. Compliance drives a large share
    of system functionality.
    <Eg>IRDAI (India), NAIC and state DOIs (US), FCA/PRA (UK), EIOPA (EU).</Eg>
  </LedgerRow>
  <LedgerRow term="Loss adjusters / Surveyors">
    Independent professionals who investigate and quantify claims — mandatory above certain
    thresholds for many Indian general claims.
  </LedgerRow>
  <LedgerRow term="InsurTechs">
    Tech-first entrants: full-stack digital insurers, distribution platforms, and enablers
    selling technology to incumbents.
    <Eg>Lemonade; in India Acko, Digit, Policybazaar, Turtlemint.</Eg>
  </LedgerRow>
  <LedgerRow term="Data & analytics providers">
    Rating content, risk data, property intelligence and catastrophe models feeding underwriting
    and pricing.
    <Eg>Verisk/ISO, LexisNexis Risk, CoreLogic, Moody's RMS.</Eg>
  </LedgerRow>
  <LedgerRow term="ACORD">
    The global standards body for insurance data exchange — forms plus AL3/XML/JSON standards.
    You will meet ACORD in nearly every broker–carrier integration.
  </LedgerRow>
</Ledger>

<SystemsNote>
Each organisation boundary is an **integration boundary with a different trust model**, and
that difference should be explicit in your design:

- A **broker** submits risk data you must validate — they act for the customer, not for you.
- An **MGA** writes business *on your paper* under delegated authority. They are not a data
  source; they are exercising your underwriting rights. That means authority limits, bordereaux
  ingestion, and after-the-fact audit rather than real-time validation.
- A **TPA** makes claim decisions on your behalf, so your reserve and payment data originates
  outside your walls and arrives on their schedule.

The common modelling error is treating all three as "external partner" with one API pattern.
</SystemsNote>
