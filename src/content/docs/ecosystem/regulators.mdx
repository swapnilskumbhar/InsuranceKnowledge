Regulators are the reason a large share of your backlog exists. This page is organised by
**what each one makes the system do**, not by country trivia.

## Who regulates where

| Market | Body | Structure |
|---|---|---|
| India | **IRDAI** | Single national regulator for insurance |
| United States | **NAIC** + state Departments of Insurance | NAIC coordinates; each state regulates and approves |
| United Kingdom | **FCA** and **PRA** | Split: FCA conduct, PRA prudential |
| European Union | **EIOPA** + national regulators | EIOPA sets direction; national bodies supervise |
| Singapore | **MAS** | Single regulator across financial services |
| Australia | **APRA** and **ASIC** | Split: APRA prudential, ASIC conduct |

Two structural patterns matter more than the names:

- **Single versus federated.** India and Singapore have one approval authority. The US has
  fifty-plus. This is the difference between a product being *a thing* and a product being *a
  matrix* — see [market structures](../../systems/market-structures/).
- **Unified versus split mandate.** The UK and Australia separate conduct supervision from
  prudential supervision. Two regulators means two reporting obligations drawing on different
  slices of the same data.

## What they force into the system

<Ledger>
  <LedgerRow term="Product filing">
    Wordings and rates approved before sale. Drives product and rate **versioning tied to
    approval state and effective date** — a filed version can never be silently edited.
    <Eg>a rate revision effective 1 April applies to business incepting after that date, while mid-term endorsements on older policies keep the old rates.</Eg>
  </LedgerRow>
  <LedgerRow term="Solvency reporting">
    Demonstrating capital adequacy — Solvency II (EU/UK), RBC (US), IRDAI's solvency margin.
    Drives reserving data quality and the ability to reproduce a figure months later.
  </LedgerRow>
  <LedgerRow term="Conduct and complaints">
    Fair treatment, disclosure at point of sale, complaint handling within deadlines. Drives
    audit trails of **what was shown to the customer and when** — not just what was stored.
  </LedgerRow>
  <LedgerRow term="Statistical returns">
    Periodic market data submissions in prescribed formats. Drives the reporting layer and, more
    painfully, retrospective consistency when definitions change.
  </LedgerRow>
  <LedgerRow term="Data residency and retention">
    Where policyholder data may physically live, and how long it must be kept. Drives hosting
    region and archival strategy — decisions that are extremely expensive to reverse.
    <Eg>a life policy sold today may need its data retained and readable for fifty years.</Eg>
  </LedgerRow>
</Ledger>

<SystemsNote>
Regulatory requirements land in the system as **non-functional requirements with functional
consequences**, and they share one property: they are all about *reproducing the past*.

Filing means you must show which approved version applied on a date. Solvency means you must
reproduce a figure after the underlying policies moved on. Conduct means you must show what a
customer was told before they bought.

A system that only stores current state cannot satisfy any of them, no matter how many reports
you bolt on afterwards. This is the same requirement that drives effective dating — regulation
is simply the reason it is not optional.
</SystemsNote>
