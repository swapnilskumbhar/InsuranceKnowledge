## The out-of-sequence problem

This is the chapter the content plan flags as the hard one, and it deserves the space.

An endorsement is not "an update to the policy." It is a **change with its own effective date**,
which may be earlier than changes already processed. When that happens, everything after it has
to be recomputed — premium, commission, earned/unearned splits, and any reinsurance cession
that depended on the sum insured.

<Example label="The shape of the problem">
A policy runs 1 Jan – 31 Dec. On 15 June an endorsement raises the sum insured, effective
1 July. On 20 June a second endorsement arrives — **effective 1 March** — changing the insured
address to a higher-rated zone.

The second endorsement is earlier than the first but processed after it. The system must
re-rate the 1 March–1 July window at the new zone, then re-apply the 1 July sum-insured change
on top of the corrected base, and produce a premium adjustment that nets against what was
already billed.

*This example states the problem. The full arithmetic — and the data structure that makes it
tractable — is the work this page still needs.*
</Example>
