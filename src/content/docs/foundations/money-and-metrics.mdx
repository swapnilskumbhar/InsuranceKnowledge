The metrics architects get asked about in the first week, and misuse for a year afterwards.

<Ledger>
  <LedgerRow term="Loss ratio">
    Claims ÷ earned premium. The core measure of underwriting quality.
  </LedgerRow>
  <LedgerRow term="Expense ratio">
    Operating expenses and commissions ÷ premium.
  </LedgerRow>
  <LedgerRow term="Combined ratio">
    Loss ratio + expense ratio. Below 100% means an underwriting profit.
  </LedgerRow>
  <LedgerRow term="GWP / NWP">
    Gross versus Net Written Premium — net is after reinsurance cessions.
  </LedgerRow>
  <LedgerRow term="Earned vs unearned">
    Premium is earned gradually across the policy period; the unearned part sits as a liability.
    <Eg>six months into an annual policy, half the premium is earned and half is still owed back if the policy cancels.</Eg>
  </LedgerRow>
  <LedgerRow term="Reserves & IBNR">
    Money held for open claims — including those Incurred But Not Reported yet.
    <Eg>a December accident claimed in February still belongs to last year's accounts; IBNR estimates it in advance.</Eg>
  </LedgerRow>
</Ledger>

<Example label="Worked example">
Earned premium ₹1,000 cr; claims ₹620 cr → loss ratio **62%**. Expenses and commissions
₹300 cr → expense ratio **30%**. Combined ratio **92%** → an underwriting profit of 8 paise
per premium rupee, before investment income. *(Illustrative figures.)*
</Example>

<SystemsNote>
**The ledger and the policy system will disagree, and this is not a bug.** The policy system
knows written premium at the moment of a transaction. Finance needs premium *earned across
time*, restated whenever an endorsement changes the policy retroactively.

Two consequences worth designing for on day one:

1. Earning is a **function of the policy's effective-dated state**, not a stored number. An
   out-of-sequence endorsement changes what was earned in a period you have already closed.
2. You need a defensible answer to "which system is right." Usually the policy system is the
   source of truth for *what was agreed* and finance is authoritative for *what was recognised*
   — and the reconciliation between them is a first-class feature, not a report someone writes
   later.
</SystemsNote>
