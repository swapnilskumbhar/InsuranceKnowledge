Most cross-market confusion is harmless: two markets use different words for one thing, you
map them once and move on. The dangerous cases are the pairs that **look** like translations
and are not — where treating them as synonyms silently corrupts the model.

This page separates the two.

## Same concept, different word

Safe to map. One field, one meaning, several labels.

| Concept | India | US | UK / London |
|---|---|---|---|
| The non-life industry | General Insurance | Property & Casualty (P&C) | General Insurance / Non-life |
| First slice borne by the insured | Excess | Deductible | Excess |
| Renewal discount for claim-free years | No Claim Bonus (NCB) | Claims-free discount | No Claims Discount (NCD) |
| Notifying a loss | Intimation / FNOL | First Notice of Loss (FNOL) | Notification / FNOL |
| Anyone who sells | Agent / Intermediary | Producer | Intermediary |
| Person who quantifies a loss | Surveyor & Loss Assessor | Adjuster | Loss adjuster |
| Mid-term change | Endorsement | Endorsement | Endorsement |

<Ledger>
  <LedgerRow term="Excess / Deductible">
    The same mechanic under two names — the amount the insured bears before the insurer pays.
    Build one field.
    <Eg>a UK motor policy's "£250 excess" and a US auto policy's "$250 deductible" are the same thing.</Eg>
  </LedgerRow>
  <LedgerRow term="Producer">
    A US umbrella term covering agents and brokers alike. It says nothing about whom the person
    legally represents, which is the distinction that actually matters.
  </LedgerRow>
</Ledger>

## Different concept, similar word

This is where the money is. These pairs are **not** interchangeable, and modelling them as one
field loses information you cannot reconstruct later.

<Ledger>
  <LedgerRow term="Sum insured vs Limit">
    Not synonyms. A **sum insured** is typically the declared value of the thing insured, and it
    drives the premium. A **limit** is the maximum the insurer will pay, and it constrains the
    claim. They coincide in simple property covers and diverge everywhere else.
    <Eg>a liability policy has a limit of ₹5 crore but no sum insured at all — there is no "value" of your legal liability to declare.</Eg>
  </LedgerRow>
  <LedgerRow term="IDV vs Agreed Value vs ACV">
    Three different answers to "what is the vehicle worth at claim time." **IDV** (India) is
    computed from a regulator-referenced depreciation schedule and fixed at inception.
    **Agreed value** is negotiated up front and paid regardless of later depreciation.
    **Actual Cash Value** (US) is assessed *at the time of loss*.
    <Eg>the same car total-lossed on the same day pays a schedule figure, a contract figure, or a market assessment — three numbers, three mechanisms.</Eg>
  </LedgerRow>
  <LedgerRow term="Agent — India/UK vs US">
    In India and the UK an agent represents the **insurer** and a broker represents the
    **customer**; the distinction is regulated and determines who is liable for bad advice. US
    usage folds both into "producer", and independent agents may hold appointments with many
    carriers.
  </LedgerRow>
  <LedgerRow term="Health insurance">
    Barely the same product across markets. India: indemnity or fixed-benefit cover, cashless
    settlement through TPAs at network hospitals. US: a payer–provider system with networks,
    Explanation of Benefits, and X12 EDI claim transactions.
    <Eg>an Indian pre-authorisation and a US prior authorisation solve superficially similar problems through entirely different data standards.</Eg>
  </LedgerRow>
</Ledger>

<Example label="Why the sum insured / limit distinction bites">
A property policy is written with sum insured ₹2 crore. A claim comes in at ₹2.4 crore.

If the schema stored one number called `limit`, the answer looks obvious: pay ₹2 crore. But
the sum insured also feeds **average** (underinsurance): if the building's actual
reconstruction cost was ₹4 crore, the insured declared half the true value, and the settlement
may be proportionately reduced — closer to ₹1.2 crore — even though the claim is under the
"limit".

Two different numbers, doing two different jobs, from one collapsed field.
*(Illustrative figures; the application of average varies by wording and market.)*
</Example>

<SystemsNote>
**Store the concept, render the label.** Field names should encode meaning, not the vocabulary
of the first market you shipped in. A column called `deductible` becomes a permanent tell that
the system was built US-first, and it will be mislabelled on every UK schedule you generate.

Three concrete rules that follow:

1. **One field for excess/deductible.** True synonyms — the difference is presentation, so make
   it a display concern driven by market, not a schema concern.
2. **Never collapse sum insured into limit.** They drive different calculations (premium versus
   settlement) and can legitimately both exist, or either exist alone.
3. **Model vehicle value as a *method*, not an amount.** `IDV`, `agreed`, and `ACV` differ in
   *when* and *how* the figure is determined. Storing only the number loses the mechanism, and
   the mechanism is what claims needs.
</SystemsNote>
